This work presented \emph{OptiMAC}, a comprehensive framework for optimizing integrity verification in lossy and adversarial communication channels. OptiMAC addresses the long-standing trade-off between security and efficiency in MAC schemes by introducing a dependency-graph representation and a mixed-integer linear programming formulation for tag-to-message assignment. Through this structured optimization, OptiMAC systematically reduces bandwidth
and computational overhead while enhancing resilience against jamming.

In contrast to traditional and heuristic-based approaches, OptiMAC moves beyond fixed or ad hoc configurations. While ProMAC established a generalized framework, it lacked a systematic method for determining critical parameters such as tag generation, update rules, and assignment strategies. OptiMAC fills this gap by mapping assignments onto a dependency graph and optimizing them mathematically, ensuring that each configuration is tailored to the demands of its environment.

Our evaluation of WiFi and 5G scenarios demonstrates that OptiMAC achieves higher effective security while operating at lower tag-to-message ratios than prior schemes. These results highlight the practicality of the framework for energy-sensitive and
mission-critical applications, where efficiency and robustness must coexist.
